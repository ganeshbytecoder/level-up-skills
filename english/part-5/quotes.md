One of the biggest differences between intermediate and advanced English speakers is the ability to recognize and appropriately use famous quotations. Native speakers often allude to these quotes without explaining them. Knowing them improves your reading, writing, speaking, and comprehension of books, movies, speeches, and interviews.

> **Note:** Because many famous quotations are copyrighted (especially from modern films, books, or songs), I'll focus mainly on classic, historical, and public-domain quotations that are commonly referenced in English.

* The truth will out.
* Love conquers all.
* United we stand, divided we fall.
* No man is an island.
* Every cloud has a silver lining.
* What doesn't kill you makes you stronger.
* The only constant is change.
* The journey of a thousand miles begins with one step.

If your goal is **near-native English**, it's also worth learning four other categories alongside quotations:

1. **Literary allusions** (e.g., *Achilles' heel*, *Pandora's box*, *Trojan horse*).
2. **Biblical references** (e.g., *the prodigal son*, *the writing on the wall*, *forbidden fruit*).
3. **Historical quotations** that appear in politics, journalism, and essays.
4. **Latin legal and academic phrases** (e.g., *de facto*, *prima facie*, *status quo*, *bona fide*, *ad hoc*).

Mastering these will help you understand many references that educated native speakers make in everyday communication.
